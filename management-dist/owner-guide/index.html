Owner Guide for New York City and New Jersey Buildings

How to check a building’s open violations, the filings and inspections owners work to each year, what a monthly owner report should show, and what to collect when changing management companies.

General information based on public agency guidance, last reviewed September 2026. Requirements depend on building size, age, occupancy, and equipment. Confirm details with the agency or a qualified professional.

Check Your Building’s Open Violations

New York City publishes violations, complaints, filings, and registration status. A few minutes on these public sites shows where a building stands.

  1. Search Housing Preservation and Development Records

    Search the address on HPD Online for housing maintenance code violations, complaints, and whether the property registration is current. Class C violations are immediately hazardous and carry the shortest correction deadlines.

    HPD Online ↗

  2. Search Department of Buildings Records

    The Building Information System lists Department of Buildings violations and complaints for the property. DOB NOW holds newer filings, including facade, boiler, and elevator compliance.

    Building Information System ↗ DOB NOW Public Portal ↗

  3. Look Up Summonses by Number

    For any summons or notice the building has received, the Office of Administrative Trials and Hearings summons finder shows its hearing status and penalty.

    Summons Finder ↗

  4. Bring the List to a Proposal Request

    Open violations, overdue filings, and upcoming inspections shape the first months of any management scope.

Compliance Calendar

Recurring New York City and New Jersey requirements for multifamily, mixed use, and commercial buildings. Which ones apply depends on the building.

Every Year in New York City

  1. January 1 to 15

    Annual Lead Paint and Window Guard Notice

    Deliver the annual notice to tenants in buildings with three or more units. Lead paint rules cover buildings built before 1960, or from 1960 to 1978 with known lead paint.

    Health Department: Window Guards ↗ Housing Preservation and Development: Annual Notice ↗

  2. By February 15

    Collect Notices and Follow Up

    Where a window guard notice is not returned, the owner must inspect the apartment. Apartments with a child under 6 need an annual lead paint inspection.

    Housing Preservation and Development: Lead Based Paint ↗

  3. May 1

    Local Law 97 Emissions Report

    Buildings over 25,000 square feet, and certain lots and condominiums over 50,000 square feet. Stricter emissions limits begin in 2030.

    Department of Buildings: Local Law 97 ↗

  4. September 1

    Property Registration

    Buildings with three or more units, and one and two family homes where neither the owner nor immediate family lives. Unregistered owners cannot bring nonpayment cases and face fines of $500 to $5,000.

    Housing Preservation and Development: Registration ↗

  5. December 1 to 31

    Bedbug Report

    Multiple dwellings file the report covering November 1 through October 31, then give tenants the receipt or post it in the building.

    Housing Preservation and Development: Bedbugs ↗

  6. Once Each Year

    Boiler, Elevator, and Backflow Testing

    Boiler inspections are filed within 14 days, including low pressure boilers in buildings with six or more families and in commercial or mixed use buildings. Elevators need an annual inspection and Category 1 test, with a Category 5 test every five years. Backflow prevention devices are tested every 12 months.

    Department of Buildings: Boilers ↗ Department of Buildings: Elevators ↗ Environmental Protection: Backflow ↗

  7. Once Each Year

    Pest and Mold Inspection

    Multiple dwellings inspect apartments for pests and mold each year and clean vacant apartments before they are rented again.

    Housing Preservation and Development: Owner Bulletin ↗

Multi Year Inspection Cycles in New York City

  1. Every 5 Years

    Facade Inspections (Local Law 11)

    Buildings taller than six stories. Cycle 10 filing windows by the last digit of the block number:

    4, 5, 6, or 9
    February 21, 2025 to February 21, 2027
    0, 7, or 8
    February 21, 2026 to February 21, 2028
    1, 2, or 3
    February 21, 2027 to February 21, 2029

    Department of Buildings: Facades ↗

  2. Every 4 Years

    Gas Piping Inspections (Local Law 152)

    Inspected by a Licensed Master Plumber and scheduled by community district. One and two family homes are exempt.

    2026
    Community districts 4, 6, 8, 9, and 16
    2027
    Community districts 11, 12, 14, 15, and 17
    2028
    Community districts 1, 3, and 10
    2029
    Community districts 2, 5, 7, 13, and 18

    Department of Buildings: Gas Piping ↗

  3. Every 6 Years, Then 4

    Parking Structure Inspections (Local Law 126)

    Initial reports for parking structures in the Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island are due December 31, 2027. The cycle shortens to four years after January 1, 2028.

    Department of Buildings: Parking Structures ↗

At Every Tenant Turnover

  1. New York City

    Vacant Apartment Preparation

    Clean vacant apartments for pests and mold before they are rented again, and complete required lead paint work on doors and windows in covered buildings.

    Housing Preservation and Development: Turnover Work ↗

  2. New York State

    Security Deposit Return

    Under state law, landlords generally have 14 days after a tenant moves out to return the deposit or provide an itemized statement of deductions.

    New York State Law: Security Deposits ↗

  3. New Jersey

    Lead Safe Inspection

    Covered rentals built before 1978 need a lead safe inspection at tenant turnover unless a valid lead safe certificate is already in place.

    Community Affairs: Lead Safe Certification ↗

New Jersey

  1. Every 3 Years

    Lead Safe Certification

    Rentals built before 1978, with limited exemptions, are inspected every three years or at tenant turnover, whichever comes first.

    Community Affairs: Lead Safe Program ↗

  2. Annually

    Landlord Registration

    Buildings with three or more units register with the state Bureau of Housing Inspection each year. One unit rentals and two unit buildings that are not owner occupied file a Landlord Identity Registration Statement with the municipal clerk. Changes are filed within 20 days.

    Community Affairs: Landlord Registration ↗

  3. Annually

    Jersey City Rent Control Registration

    Buildings with five or more units register each year with the Office of Landlord/Tenant Relations.

    Jersey City: Landlord/Tenant Relations ↗

What a Monthly Owner Report Should Show

Whoever manages the building, these are the sections an owner should be able to review each month. Format and timing are set in the management agreement.

Collections and Arrears

Rent roll, payments received, balances by tenant, and follow up on arrears.

Expenses Against Budget

Paid invoices by category compared with the operating budget.

Bank Reconciliations

Operating and security deposit accounts reconciled to bank statements.

Work Orders

Requests opened and closed, and open items with their current status.

Violations and Deadlines

Open violations, upcoming filings and inspections, and correction dates.

Leasing and Capital Projects

Vacancies, renewals, lease expirations, and project budgets and progress.

Checklist for Changing Management Companies

Items to collect from the outgoing manager. Timing depends on the current agreement and record availability.

  • Current management agreement and termination notice terms
  • Operating and security deposit bank accounts, with signatory changes
  • Rent roll, tenant ledgers, and arrears detail
  • Leases, renewals, and rent stabilization records where applicable
  • Security deposit records by tenant
  • Vendor and service contracts
  • Vendor insurance certificates
  • Open work orders and warranties
  • Open violations and pending filings or inspections
  • Utility, property tax, and water accounts
  • Keys, access codes, and building system information
  • Tenant contact records and the notice of the change
