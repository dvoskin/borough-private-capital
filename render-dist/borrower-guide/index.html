Before You Sign a Private Real Estate Loan

What lenders commonly request, how renovation draws work, what can change before closing, and the fees worth asking about in writing.

  • Documents
  • Renovation Draws
  • Before Closing
  • Fees

General information for business purpose investment property loans. It is not a quote, loan terms, or legal or tax advice.

Documents Lenders Commonly Request

Requirements vary by lender, property, and borrower. Having these ready shortens underwriting. They are requested after an initial review, not through the website brief.

Every Request

For the borrowing entity and its guarantors.

  • Articles of organization and operating agreement
  • Employer Identification Number letter
  • Certificate of good standing
  • Experience with similar projects
  • Property insurance quote
  • Title company contact

Purchases and Refinances

For the property and the transaction.

  • Signed contract of sale
  • Proof of funds for equity and closing costs
  • Payoff letter for existing debt
  • Settlement statement from the original purchase

Renovation Projects

For fix and flip and value add plans.

  • Scope of work with a line item budget
  • Contractor bids
  • Project timeline
  • Comparable sales supporting the after repair value

Rental Properties

For income producing assets.

  • Rent roll
  • Current leases
  • Trailing twelve month operating statement
  • Property tax and insurance bills

How Renovation Draws Commonly Work

Many renovation loans hold back construction funds at closing and release them in stages after completed work is verified. Knowing the process before signing helps keep a project from running short of cash.

  1. Funds Held Back at Closing

    The renovation portion of the loan is set aside instead of paid out at closing.

  2. Work Completed and Draw Requested

    The borrower requests a draw for work finished under the approved budget.

  3. Work Verified

    An inspector, or photos and video, confirms what was completed.

  4. Funds Released

    Funds are sent for verified work. Many lenders reimburse completed work instead of advancing funds.

Questions to Ask Any Lender About Draws

Get the answers in writing before you sign.

  • Are renovation funds advanced, or reimbursed after completion?
  • How long from a draw request to inspection, and from inspection to funds?
  • Is there a fee per draw, per inspection, or per wire?
  • Is partially completed work funded?
  • How many draws are allowed, and is there a minimum amount?
  • What happens when the budget or scope changes?

Because draws are often paid after work is completed, plan enough working capital to pay contractors before each release.

What Can Change Between a Quote and Closing

An early quote reflects the information available at the time. These are common reasons final terms differ from it.

Valuation

An appraisal or after repair value below the estimate can reduce the loan amount.

Leverage

Final loan to cost and loan to value limits are set in underwriting.

Title

Liens, judgments, open permits, or unpaid charges must be resolved before closing.

Insurance

Required coverage, and its cost, is confirmed before closing.

Entity Documents

Missing or inconsistent entity and guarantor documents can delay closing.

Pricing

Rates and fees that are not locked can change with the market or the final loan profile.

Compare the Term Sheet With the Closing Statement

Line up both documents before you sign and ask about any difference.

  • Loan amount and renovation holdback
  • Interest rate, and whether interest accrues on undrawn funds
  • Points and every listed fee
  • Term, extension options, and extension fees
  • Prepayment terms and any minimum interest
  • Draw, inspection, and wire fees
  • Default interest and late charges
  • Interest, tax, and insurance reserves

At Borough Private Capital, pricing, leverage, fees, and timing depend on underwriting, eligibility, documentation, and funding availability. An inquiry is not an approval or commitment to lend.

Fees to Ask About

Not every loan carries every fee. Ask for each one in writing before you commit.

Origination Points
A percentage of the loan amount charged at closing. One point equals 1% of the loan.
Underwriting, Processing, or Document Fees
Flat charges for reviewing the request and preparing loan documents.
Appraisal or Valuation Fee
The cost of valuing the property, often paid before closing and not always refundable.
Draw, Inspection, and Wire Fees
Charges tied to each renovation draw, inspection, or transfer of funds.
Extension Fee
A charge to extend the loan past its original maturity date, often a percentage of the loan.
Exit Fee
A charge some lenders apply when the loan is repaid.
Prepayment Penalty or Minimum Interest
A charge when a loan is repaid before a set date or before a minimum amount of interest is earned.
Default Interest and Late Charges
Higher interest and fees that apply after a missed payment or a missed maturity date.
Legal, Title, and Recording Costs
Lender attorney fees, title insurance, and government recording charges.
Broker Fee
Compensation paid to a mortgage broker, which should be disclosed in writing.
